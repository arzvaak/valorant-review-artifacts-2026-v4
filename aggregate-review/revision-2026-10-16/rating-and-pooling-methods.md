### Rating state and numerical updates

The exact Glicko-2 reference is Glickman's 22 March 2022 technical specification, [Example of the Glicko-2 System](https://glicko.net/glicko/glicko2.pdf). Original-scale ratings/deviations convert as $\mu=(r-1500)/173.7178$, $\phi=RD/173.7178$. Initialize $r=1500$, $RD=350$, $\sigma=0.06$; volatility constraint $\tau=0.5$ and root tolerance $10^{-6}$. States for map and series outcomes are separate. Equal-release batches define periods. Before an update after elapsed $d$ days, use $\widetilde\phi^2=\phi^2+\sigma^2\max(d-1,0)$; the published period step then adds the updated volatility variance. At prediction, integrate elapsed $\phi^2+\sigma^2\max(d,0)$ without changing the stored mean. A never-updated state has zero elapsed inflation. This is an elapsed-day adaptation with sparse periods, rather than a claim to follow the specification's suggested games-per-period schedule.

For opponents $j$, let $g_j=(1+3\widetilde\phi_j^2/\pi^2)^{-1/2}$ and $E_j=\operatorname{sigmoid}(g_j(\mu-\mu_j))$. Then $v=[\sum_jg_j^2E_j(1-E_j)]^{-1}$, $\Delta=v\sum_jg_j(y_j-E_j)$. Solve the published volatility equation

$$f(x)=\frac{e^x(\Delta^2-\widetilde\phi^2-v-e^x)}{2(\widetilde\phi^2+v+e^x)^2}-\frac{x-\log\sigma^2}{\tau^2}=0.$$

Set $\sigma'=e^{x/2}$, $\phi_*^2=\widetilde\phi^2+\sigma'^2$, $\phi'^2=(\phi_*^{-2}+v^{-1})^{-1}$ and $\mu'=\mu+\phi'^2\sum_jg_j(y_j-E_j)$. All simultaneous observations use frozen pre-batch means/deviations. Predictions average the two directed team expectations, so swapping sides complements the forecast exactly. Failed root bracketing/convergence is an explicit failure rather than a substituted CPU or fixed-volatility result.

The player reference uses the Gaussian team-sum model of [Herbrich, Minka and Graepel (2006)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2006/file/f44ee263952e65b3610b8ba51229d1f9-Paper.pdf), with diagonal assumed-density updates. Initialize each task-specific player skill at mean 25 and variance $(25/3)^2$; performance variance is $(25/6)^2$ per player, and add $(25/300)^2d$ skill variance for elapsed days. For two five-player teams, $c^2=\sum_{i=1}^{10}s_i^2+10\beta^2$ and $p=\Phi[(\sum_A m_i-\sum_Bm_i)/c]$. With observed direction $q=2y-1$, let $t=q(\sum_A m_i-\sum_Bm_i)/c$, $V(t)=\varphi(t)/\Phi(t)$ and $W(t)=V(t)[V(t)+t]$. Each player's posterior is

$$m_i'=m_i+q_i\frac{s_i^2}{c}V(t),\qquad s_i'^2=s_i^2\left[1-\frac{s_i^2}{c^2}W(t)\right],$$

where $q_i=q$ on A and $-q$ on B. Log-normal-CDF evaluation stabilizes tail ratios; $W$ is clipped to $[0,1-10^{-9}]$. Simultaneous posterior messages combine as precision/natural-mean increments from a common pre-batch state. This is not full iterative expectation propagation over a multi-game graph. Map rosters are the observed ten players; series rosters are the last unambiguous released map appearances, frozen at prediction. Incomplete series proxies use team Glicko-2; full but unrated rosters retain their initial Gaussian uncertainty. Actual future starting lineups are never substituted.

K20 updates both team ratings with signed $20(y-p)$ increments using the stored pre-prediction probability. Equal-release increments are summed. The original core map-derived series rating remains separate from these task-specific map/series states. All references exclude known globals from updates. Six-hour online state admits only earlier valid permitted outcomes; frozen state admits training outcomes only. The two-hour frozen sensitivity retains the original core delay as a distinct procedure. Common calibration fits each procedure's nonnegative scalar logit scale on its original separate calibration events, with identity/chance candidates and the minimum five-event/two-class rule. Parameters are fixed without evaluation tuning. All calculations use PyTorch float64 on CUDA; no external CPU Glicko/TrueSkill package is represented as GPU execution.

### Controlled player tensors, pooling and fitting

Only six side-headshot channels are corrected: attack/defence headshot percentage in overall, recent and linked histories. Each block queries only its admitted historical training Game IDs, preserving historical player/agent/map links and missing/conflict states. Block 12 has 993,758 non-null values among 1,013,480 historical side cells. Other arrays are unchanged. Original overall numeric headshot history was already retained.

BN, BNC, BNL and BNLC independently cross general, composition and linked channels; C additionally permits player agent categories. All corrected conditions share the same player values, masks, roster proxies, phase memberships and preprocessing rules. Two 64-unit ReLU/dropout-0.1 layers encode each player. Sum, mean or single-query softmax attention pool each side to 64 dimensions; their two-layer 128-unit prediction heads match. Summary pooling concatenates means, population standard deviations, maxima and top-two means of those same encoded player tensors, giving a wider 256-dimensional per-side head input. Its parameter-count difference is explicit.

AdamW uses learning rate 0.0003 and weight decay 0.001, at most 64 epochs, stopping patience eight and minimum log-loss improvement $10^{-6}$. Training mirrors both teams/labels and inference symmetrizes them. Three seeds (20261009, 20261010, 20261011) are averaged before separate-event scalar calibration. CUDA bfloat16 mixed precision uses float32 logits/loss. Initial batch 1,024 can halve to 128 only after a preserved OOM attempt. The recipe is fixed across conditions; it is not individually optimized architecture search. Sparse blocks retain the original K20 fallback and stay in denominators. Every epoch checkpoint and measured batch/time/memory receipt is retained.

All 408 block/task/condition packages freeze before evaluation scoring. Eight eligible blocks yield 816 actual fitted seeds; four sparse blocks retain fallback packages. Pool contrasts comprise mean−sum, attention−sum, attention−mean, sum−summary and attention−summary across four representations, two tasks and two losses (80). Channel contrasts compare BNC−BN, BNLC−BNL, BNL−BN and BNLC−BNC across four pools/tasks/losses (64). Corrected versus original BNLC sum under the identical recipe gives four projection contrasts. Each family has its own 131,072-draw centered-event-bootstrap/Monte-Carlo-bound/Holm calculation, seed 20261020 plus stable contrast ordinal. No original family is replaced.

### Retuned conditional-removal sensitivity

Each supported block/task retains the original earlier-selected full-input parent's architecture. Each of nine semantic drops and the coverage-only condition independently searches that architecture's original preset grid, using the original seeds, maximum epochs, stopping, selection and calibration partitions. Reduced preprocessing fits from permitted training rows only. The full reference is the original already-searched parent. All new predictions freeze before evaluation scoring. The 40 new contrasts use 131,072 complete-event draws, seed 20261019 plus contrast ordinal, and a separate Monte Carlo/Holm family. This is conditional reliance with retuning, not an attribution of independent causal information.
