An earlier-selection ensemble-and-threshold decision layer, reusing the five-family core procedures, K20 and the orientation-sensitive training prior, reaches 61.04% ordinary map and 59.92% series accuracy without a new base architecture. Its series advantage over the training prior is 1.60 row-weighted percentage points but only 0.12 event-weighted points, with an interval crossing zero. Thus metric weighting changes the apparent gain. It selects single arms or probability blends and a threshold on earlier selection events; it is not one fixed base model with a changed threshold. Non-0.5 thresholds and prior-containing blends can make decisions depend on canonical orientation. The retained thresholds range over the fixed 0.35--0.65 grid in 0.025 steps, using raw mixture probabilities. Relative to applying 0.5 to those same mixtures, thresholds change 6,904 map and 2,701 series decisions. Under swapping symmetric components while retaining the prior scalar, 21,177 map and 12,716 series decisions do not complement. This is a canonical-orientation decision policy, rather than a symmetric probability-quality improvement.

The compact published-method adaptation uses prior-roster ACS and source-performance-rating differences, historical attack/defense win fractions and four official agent-role-count differences. Series roles use each proxy player's most-observed earlier agent. Its stack combines XGBoost, CatBoost, Gaussian naive Bayes and a histogram-tree adaptation, with a regularized logistic meta-model fitted on earlier stopping forecasts, rather than the unavailable published out-of-fold procedure. The fixed chronological diagnostic uses depth three, seed 20261010, threshold .5 and raw probabilities. Without usable two-class stopping data, the fixed stack reuses XGBoost; if training lacks both classes, both arms use the training prior.

On exactly matched 2025 identities (6,126 maps, 2,523 series), a fixed compact XGBoost adaptation attains ordinary row-weighted 60.48% chronological versus 60.69% mixed-date five-fold map accuracy, and 61.12% versus 60.84% for series. The corresponding stack ranges from 60.04% to 61.75% across tasks/policies. These diagnostics neither reproduce nor refute the published 75.72% : input inventory, stopping and meta-fitting are different. The mixed-date comparison is descriptive because later observations may enter training. These are different estimands and do not select the final procedure.
